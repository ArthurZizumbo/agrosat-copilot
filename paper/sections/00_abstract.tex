\begin{abstract}
Accurate quantification of cultivated land without physical field
inspection is a high-impact challenge for the agri-tech sector.
We present AgroSatCopilot, an open-source conversational system that
maps crop types from multi-temporal Sentinel-2 imagery and explains its
predictions in natural language. The perception stack combines dense
semantic segmentation over satellite image time series with tabular
classifiers built on AlphaEarth Foundations annual
embeddings~\cite{brown2025alphaearth} and a contrastive
vision-language branch (FarSLIP)~\cite{li2025farslip}. On the PASTIS-R
benchmark (18 crop classes, metropolitan
France)~\cite{garnot2021pastis}, our best individual model,
TSViT-pheno~\cite{tarasiou2023tsvit}, reaches a mean intersection over
union (mIoU) of 0.6253 and macro F1 of 0.7500 on the held-out fold,
and a heterogeneous stacking ensemble of five members attains an
out-of-fold macro F1 of 0.6477. The conversational layer follows the
\emph{Be My Eyes} pattern~\cite{huang2025bemyeyes}: the team's models
\emph{perceive} each parcel and emit plain-text observations, while a
frozen frontier reasoner --- Gemini~2.5~Pro in the cloud or
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on-premises for data sovereignty --- reasons over those
observations, invokes geospatial tools, and answers. The reasoner is
never used as a pixel classifier. We further study spatial
transferability: a France-trained segmenter is zero-shot inapplicable
to Catalonia (Sen4AgriNet)~\cite{sykas2022sen4agrinet} but recovers a
mIoU of 0.2468 after few-shot fine-tuning, consistent with reported
limits on the transferability of foundation-model
embeddings~\cite{harvesting2026alphaearth}. All datasets, code, and
figures are released under permissive licenses for reproducibility.
\end{abstract}
