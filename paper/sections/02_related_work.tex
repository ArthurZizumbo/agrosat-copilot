\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Foundation models for Earth observation}
AlphaEarth Foundations~\cite{brown2025alphaearth} provides global,
analysis-ready annual embeddings, distributed on Google Earth Engine as
the asset \texttt{SATELLITE\_EMBEDDING/V1/ANNUAL} (data version~1.1,
64-dimensional, CC-BY-4.0). We use these embeddings directly as tabular
features rather than training a foundation model of our own. DINOv3 in
its satellite variant~\cite{oquab2023dinov3} contributes
self-supervised frozen features. AnySat~\cite{astruc2024anysat} is a
multi-resolution EO model that we evaluate as a segmentation backbone.
For fine-grained, language-aligned understanding,
FarSLIP~\cite{li2025farslip} adapts CLIP to remote sensing and supplies
the contrastive vision-language branch used here. Recent work on the
spatial transferability of these embeddings reports that performance
degrades across regions and that few-shot adaptation is
necessary~\cite{harvesting2026alphaearth}; our transfer experiments
corroborate this.

\subsection{Segmentation of satellite image time series}
Temporal attention models established the state of the art on
parcel-level crop mapping over image time series. U-TAE introduced a
lightweight temporal self-attention encoder for
SITS~\cite{garnot2021utae}, and the PASTIS / PASTIS-R benchmark
formalised panoptic and semantic evaluation with spatially disjoint
folds~\cite{garnot2021pastis}. TSViT~\cite{tarasiou2023tsvit} factorises
attention along the temporal and spatial axes and is the strongest
backbone in our study; we extend it with a phenological branch
(TSViT-pheno) inspired by phenology-aware
transformers~\cite{wen2025phenology}. As spatial-only baselines we
include U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet},
DeepLabv3+~\cite{chen2018deeplabv3plus}, and
SegFormer~\cite{xie2021segformer} (B0, RGB three-band variant). We note
that, in contrast to earlier project plans, no volumetric Swin-UNETR
model was trained; AnySat~\cite{astruc2024anysat} occupies the
foundation-model slot in our comparison.

\subsection{Vision-language models for agriculture}
Multimodal benchmarks such as AgroMind~\cite{ruan2025agromind} probe
agricultural remote-sensing understanding through question answering.
AgroMind is evaluation-only (no training split, roughly 28{,}482 QA
pairs); fine-tuning on it would induce data leakage, so we use it
strictly for evaluation of the conversational layer. We rely on open
language models --- Gemma~\cite{team2024gemma} and
Qwen3~\cite{yang2025qwen3} (both Apache~2.0) --- and on the frontier
Gemini~2.5~Pro reasoner~\cite{google2025gemini25} for cloud operation.

\subsection{Grounded LLM agents}
The \emph{Be My Eyes} pattern~\cite{huang2025bemyeyes} decouples
perception from reasoning: perceivers emit textual observations and a
frozen reasoner answers, which bounds hallucination because every cited
figure originates in an auditable tool result. We adopt this pattern
verbatim: our segmentation and tabular models are the perceivers, and
the LLM is a frozen reasoner that never classifies pixels. We further
combine a PostGIS spatial pre-filter with pgvector semantic search
(Spatial-RAG) to ground responses in real neighbouring parcels.
